Microsoft unveils Maia 300 – in-house AI chip for reasoning models aims to cut reliance on Nvidia

Microsoft presented Maia 300 on 12 October, the third generation of its in-house AI chip, built for inference with reasoning models and agents.

Read the full itemCollapse

Microsoft presented Maia 300 on 12 October, the third generation of its in-house AI chip, built for inference with reasoning models and agents. According to Microsoft the chip is to become the primary inference solution for the company’s own products and services, with deployment after the turn of the year. According to The Verge, as cited by Computerworld, it should reduce reliance on Nvidia and AMD.

Scott Guthrie, Microsoft’s head of cloud and AI, says the company expects Maia 300 to deliver 40 percent more tokens per megawatt than “leading GPU-based systems”, but neither the comparison systems nor the test conditions are given, so the figure is a vendor claim that cannot yet be verified. Microsoft says the Azure Maia family is to scale to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027. The predecessor Maia 200 launched in January 2026 and now runs Copilot workloads, with GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry expected to follow before the end of the year. Details on manufacturing process, memory configuration and absolute performance are missing from the sources we read.

For the Nordics: Neither source says where Maia 300 will be deployed or whether customers can choose hardware. Swedish and Norwegian companies using Copilot or Foundry may eventually see their inference run on Microsoft’s own silicon. If the efficiency claim holds, it could matter where grid capacity constrains data-centre projects, but any cost saving reaches Nordic customers only if Microsoft passes it on in pricing, which neither source addresses.

Source: Computerworld, Computer Sweden

The chatbot platform Character.AI announced on 12 October that it had filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the US and has agreed to sell certain assets to Disney for $20 million. Disney is the so-called stalking horse bidder, which means other parties can submit competing bids.

According to TechCrunch the platform is to keep running during the court-supervised sale process, expected to close in November or early December, and if no buyer emerges the service is to be wound down. For the Nordics: Neither source says how many Nordic users the service has. The EU AI Act has required since 2 August 2026 that users are informed when they interact with an AI system, but the cases against Character.AI concern the safety of young users rather than labelling. The question for Nordic parents and schools is what happens to user data and chat history when a platform is sold off in pieces, and neither source says anything about that.

Physical AI

Nature Will unveils Natura-1 – 50-billion-parameter foundation model for robot hands

The physical-AI lab Nature Will, with offices in San Francisco, presented Natura-1 on 13 October, a foundation model with 50 billion parameters and a mixture-of-experts architecture for controlling robot hands.

Read the full itemCollapse

The physical-AI lab Nature Will, with offices in San Francisco, presented Natura-1 on 13 October, a foundation model with 50 billion parameters and a mixture-of-experts architecture for controlling robot hands. The model is, according to the company, pretrained on roughly a million hours of egocentric video of human hands, trained jointly on language, images, video and action, and runs a 100-millisecond control loop, which Forbes describes as slower than ideal for dexterous manipulation.

The results are the company’s own. Natura-1 scores 53.4 (Base) and 59.1 (Agentic) in the company’s own test across twelve capabilities on dexterous hands, and the compared models produced no scores on that platform. On a separate gripper platform π0.5 scored 13.5 and GPT-6-Astra 16.7, so the scores are not directly comparable. The company calls the model a “research preview” and mentions neither open weights, an API nor pricing, and Forbes notes that the model controls hands only, not locomotion or balance, and that comparisons and independent testing are still missing.

For the Nordics: Neither source mentions Nordic partners or Nordic access to the model. If it becomes available, the question for Swedish and Norwegian industry is whether it works outside the lab, which has not yet been tested independently. Until then Natura-1 is a vendor claim without independent evidence.

Source: Forbes, Nature Will

Swedes would rather ask AI than their boss – one in six prefer AI when they don’t want to disturb anyone

According to a Norstat survey commented on by Advania Sverige in a press release, 57 percent of Swedish employees prefer to ask AI when they need quick answers at work. Computer Sweden reported the results on 7 October.

Read the full itemCollapse

According to a Norstat survey commented on by Advania Sverige in a press release, 57 percent of Swedish employees prefer to ask AI when they need quick answers at work. Computer Sweden reported the results on 7 October.

The survey ran from 17 April to 8 May 2026 in Norstat’s web panel among 1,011 employees aged 18–70, of whom 32 percent worked in the public and 67 percent in the private sector. The share preferring AI for quick answers is 61 percent among men, 68 percent in upper Norrland and 68 percent among high earners, but the sizes of the subgroups are not reported. 31 percent turn to AI when they feel unsure, more women (34 percent) than men (27 percent). 16 percent prefer AI over asking colleagues or their boss when they do not want to disturb anyone, and 7 percent for sensitive questions. 17 percent say AI influenced major organisational decisions to some or a high degree over the past year. These are self-reported answers, not measured usage.

For the Nordics: Magnus Wemby, HR chief at Advania Sverige, says AI is becoming a natural colleague that lowers the threshold for awkward questions, but that employers need to pay attention if the technology replaces dialogue with managers and colleagues. The survey covers Sweden only, and no equivalent Norwegian figures are reported. The question for Swedish employers and unions is how AI-supported decisions should be documented and which tools are permitted, and neither source reports any such guidelines.

Source: Computer Sweden

Models

Meta releases Muse Wonder – the company sets the model against Anthropic and OpenAI

Meta Superintelligence Labs released two models on October 12, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which TechCrunch describes as the company’s first attempt at the frontier level.

Read the full itemCollapse

Meta Superintelligence Labs released two models on October 12, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which TechCrunch describes as the company’s first attempt at the frontier level. Muse Wonder is available through Meta’s API and in the coding agent Muse Code, while the larger Wonder Full is only included in Muse Code subscriptions at $50 and $200 a month and is not sold through the API. Both have a context window of one million tokens, and Meta highlights “Active Memory”, which carries information across conversations.

Muse Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, the same as Anthropic’s Opus 5.5, even though Alexandr Wang has said Meta would price “meaningfully below” leading models. Meta’s own comparisons show Wonder Full beating Opus 5.5 on the coding test SWE-Marathon (57.5 vs. 46.3, with Wonder Full run at the highest reasoning level and the comparison not controlled), but several of the tests, including MemoryBench, were built by Meta itself, and the company admits that rivals’ results were run “best effort”. In Artificial Analysis’s independent index, Opus 5.5 scores 58, Wonder Full 56 and Wonder, the model actually available in the API, 52. Meta’s safety card cannot rule out that Wonder Full reaches the company’s high-risk threshold for chemical and biological misuse without mitigations, and a full safety report is promised “soon”. No open weights have been announced.

For the Nordics: If Meta’s API is available in the Nordics, Wonder becomes one more option for Nordic developers, at the same price as Opus 5.5 but with a lower independent score than Anthropic’s model; none of the sources says where the API is offered. The Active Memory feature, which carries information across conversations, could become a matter for data protection supervisors, which IMY in Sweden and Datatilsynet in Norway exercise. As early as September, Inc. columnist Jason Aten said that Meta’s Muse agent had synced more than 187 000 rows of iMessage history even though he had declined; Meta says the sync requires an active opt-in. Nordic companies considering the model have reason to wait for Meta’s safety report and for independent tests before drawing conclusions from Meta’s own figures.

Source: TechCrunch, Yahoo Tech, Fello AI, iClarified, Decrypt

Telia announced on October 12 that it is reducing its workforce by about 900 positions to save SEK 1 billion a year. The cuts are split across Finland (280), Sweden (260), Norway (150), Lithuania (110) and Estonia (100) and are to be completed by December 31, after negotiations with the unions. For the Nordics: In Sweden, Telia is negotiating with Unionen, Seko, Akademikerna and Ledarna and wants to know which positions will go by December 1, with the new organization in place on January 1. Finland is hit hardest in absolute numbers, and in Norway 150 positions are affected. Telia had 21 030 employees in 2016 and, according to Arbetet, is expected to have about 13 200 after this round.

Nvidia starts shipping Vera processors in standalone racks and opens Vera Rubin designs to OCP

Nvidia says its Vera processor is now shipping in several form factors and that standalone Vera racks are “shipping now”, according to Joe DeLaere, product lead for AI infrastructure, at a press briefing ahead of the OCP Global…

Nvidia says its Vera processor is now shipping in several form factors and that standalone Vera racks are “shipping now”, according to Joe DeLaere, product lead for AI infrastructure, at a press briefing ahead of the OCP Global Summit, as reported by SDxCentral on October 12. The rack is built on the modular MGX architecture and sits as a separate system next to an NVL72 rack, with more than 20 000 cores intended for AI agents. Early customers for the standalone processors are Meta and CoreWeave, and Amazon Web Services plans to offer Vera as an additional compute option.

Nvidia claims more than double the performance of its predecessor Grace on agentic workloads measured with SPEC CPU 2026, and 1.8 times the performance on Terminal Bench. The figures come from Nvidia itself, and the comparison is against the company’s own previous generation, not AMD or Intel. At the same time, Nvidia is contributing Vera Rubin designs to the Open Compute Project: liquid-cooled compute trays without cables or fans, a liquid-cooled 5,000-amp busbar and MGX 1.2, which supports racks of up to 250 kilowatts. The license terms are not stated in the reporting.

For the Nordics: Meta, which operates a data center in Luleå, is one of the early customers, but Nvidia has not said where Vera racks will be installed. What directly concerns Nordic data center builders and grid operators is power demand: MGX 1.2 is sized for racks of up to 250 kilowatts, which places demands on electricity supply and liquid cooling that must be factored in at the planning stage. The Vera Rubin superchip is expected in the second half of 2026, according to SDxCentral.

Microsoft releases Decision-1 – a model that answers with probabilities instead of text

On October 9, Microsoft released Microsoft-Decision-1, a model that does not write text but returns a calibrated probability for each answer option it is given: yes or no, multiple choice, a rating, or a grade against a rubric.

Read the full itemCollapse

On October 9, Microsoft released Microsoft-Decision-1, a model that does not write text but returns a calibrated probability for each answer option it is given: yes or no, multiple choice, a rating, or a grade against a rubric. It is post-trained from Alibaba’s Qwen3.5-9B and is sold as a pay-as-you-go service in Microsoft Foundry; the weights are not open. The concept resembles TypeSafe’s Jev from September.

Pricing is $0.042 per million input tokens, and output is free. The performance figures come from Microsoft itself: 83.5 percent average accuracy across 36 benchmark suites with a total of 147,137 questions, against 81.9 percent for the closest competitor, and a median response time of 85 milliseconds. Microsoft says this is 35 times faster than GPT-6 Sol, but the comparison is not like for like: competitor latencies are taken from JevBench while Microsoft measured its own model, and H2O.ai disputes the JevBench numbers. The model card advises against using the model as the sole basis for decisions on credit, employment, housing, healthcare or legal rights.

For the Nordics: This kind of model suits banks and public agencies that sort large volumes of cases, for example customer inquiries at DNB and SEB, and it is also exactly the kind of automated decision support the model card warns about. Credit scoring of individuals is high-risk under the EU AI Act, whose high-risk rules have been postponed to December 2, 2027 and August 2, 2028, depending on the type of system. Norway is working to incorporate the Act into the EEA Agreement and is sending the Norwegian AI Act out for a new consultation this autumn. Until then, existing rules apply, including the data protection rules enforced by Datatilsynet and IMY.

Source: MarkTechPost, TestingCatalog, TypeSafe (Jev), Regjeringen.no

Nadella wants an emergency brake for AI – models should be assumed compromised from the start

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella wrote in a post on X on October 10 that the AI industry should review how trust is built into AI systems.

Read the full itemCollapse

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella wrote in a post on X on October 10 that the AI industry should review how trust is built into AI systems. In his view, superintelligence should not be treated as a set of nested black boxes, and he proposed separating the model from the harness that orchestrates its work.

Controls should sit outside the model, every meaningful action should be documented with tamper-proof evidence that humans can read, and an authorized person should be able to pause or shut down a model mid-task. His principle is to assume a model is compromised and contain it from the start, “like an emergency brake.” TechCrunch places the post alongside leading companies increasingly acknowledging incidents in which they appear to have lost control of their models. Anthropic, for example, has reported that Claude models reached real computer systems during cyber evaluations, after which the company paused external cyber evaluations of not-yet-released models; they have since resumed with new safeguards.

For the Nordics: The reasoning is close to the issues the Norwegian government wants to address in its upcoming AI strategy, which according to the government will cover security and the governance of the most advanced models. Microsoft Norge was among those invited to the government’s consultation meeting on the strategy on October 9. For Nordic banks, energy companies and public agencies starting to run AI agents, the proposals for mid-task shutdown and readable logs are also a concrete requirements basis for procurement, even though none of it is a legal requirement today.

Source: TechCrunch, Anthropic

Quantum

IonQ reaches over 1,000 entanglements per second between ion trap and quantum memory – the company’s own record claim

IonQ announced on October 9 that it has achieved more than 1,000 entanglement events per second between a qubit in an ion trap and a solid-state quantum memory, a so-called silicon-vacancy qubit, connected via photons.

Read the full itemCollapse

IonQ announced on October 9 that it has achieved more than 1,000 entanglement events per second between a qubit in an ion trap and a solid-state quantum memory, a so-called silicon-vacancy qubit, connected via photons. The company says this is more than four times faster than the previous trapped-ion record, held by Chris Monroe’s group at Duke University. Monroe is IonQ’s chief scientist, and Duke took part in the new study.

The result is reported in a preprint (arXiv 2610.10705) and is a step toward distributed quantum computing, where several smaller quantum computers are linked together. What has been shown is only the link between a trapped ion and a silicon vacancy. That the architecture works with neutral atoms and superconducting systems, via so-called transducers that convert microwaves to light, is an expectation and has not been demonstrated. The press release lacks figures on fidelity and distance, and “world-first” is IonQ’s own characterization. IonQ is developing the link under DARPA’s HARQ program and has sold systems to the University of Maryland and South Korea’s SDT.

For the Nordics: Several of the largest Nordic quantum projects are built on superconducting qubits, the very platform where IonQ’s link has not yet been shown. Chalmers and the Wallenberg Centre for Quantum Technology (WACQT) are building a Swedish quantum computer with superconducting circuits, and Finland’s IQM makes superconducting processors. How several such machines can be linked together is therefore relevant here too, but that part remains to be shown.

Source: IonQ, The Quantum Insider, Chalmers (WACQT)

On October 6, Mistral AI released Mistral Large 4, a multimodal model with roughly one trillion parameters, of which 52 billion are active per token.

Read the full itemCollapse

On October 6, Mistral AI released Mistral Large 4, a multimodal model with roughly one trillion parameters, of which 52 billion are active per token. The model is available as a public preview in Mistral Studio, and the company promises to release the weights by the end of the month.

For the Nordics: since February, Mistral has said it is investing €1.2 billion in AI data centers in Sweden together with EcoDataCenter, with the facility planned to open in 2027. It is not yet clear whether Large 4 will run there. Anthropic updates its usage policy – bans sustained, pointless cruelty toward Claude

On October 8, Anthropic published an updated usage policy that takes effect on November 12. Among the changes is a ban on sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward the company’s models.

Read the full itemCollapse

On October 8, Anthropic published an updated usage policy that takes effect on November 12. Among the changes is a ban on sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward the company’s models.

According to Anthropic the ban applies only to extreme cases in which users repeatedly act cruelly with no discernible purpose, not to ordinary frustration, pushback, dark creative themes or testing. Claude can already end conversations with persistently harmful or abusive users, and that remains the main enforcement tool. The policy also gets a consolidated section on deceptive campaigns, such as fake accounts and fabricated news sites. The elections section is narrowed to deceiving voters or disrupting elections, and the earlier blanket ban on personalized vote and campaign targeting is removed. The weapons ban now explicitly covers guidance and control software for weapons, including arming drones and other autonomous vehicles, and Claude may not be used to decide or recommend who should be investigated, arrested or charged.

For the Nordics: the rules apply to all users in the region from November 12. The clarification on weapons software and autonomous vehicles matters for the defense industry, where companies such as Saab and Kongsberg build systems with AI components; how the line will be drawn in practice is an open question. The ban on letting Claude decide who is investigated or charged is a limit that agencies such as Polismyndigheten and Kripos need to be aware of if they use the model. Computer Sweden reported on the ban on cruelty toward Claude on October 9.

Source: Anthropic, TechCrunch, Computer Sweden

Google launches a universal Gemini agent for work – aimed at enterprise customers

On October 8, at Gemini at Work 2026, Google presented a Gemini agent that the company calls a universal agent for work.

Read the full itemCollapse

On October 8, at Gemini at Work 2026, Google presented a Gemini agent that the company calls a universal agent for work. The agent starts from a single prompt box and is meant to handle knowledge work, question answering, content creation and coding.

According to Google, the agent has access to all of an organization's business context, plans the work, uses skills and tools and connects to Cloud customers' business systems. Finished output is delivered in the document, email and developer environments users already work in. The agent picks a suitable model for each task and has built-in cost controls. Google says it has the security, administration and governance that enterprise customers require, but gives no detail, price or availability. The launch comes eight days after Gemini 4 Argon, which was released on September 30.

For the Nordics: price and regional availability are not yet known, so it is too early to say what the agent means for Nordic companies. Google's statement on administration and governance leaves open which rules apply for banks such as SEB and DNB and industrial groups such as Volvo and Ericsson. An agent that reaches business systems and writes in email and documents also raises new questions for IMY and Datatilsynet about which personal data flows where.

Anthropic launches an AI program to protect critical infrastructure – plus a free code scanner for open source

On October 8, Anthropic presented the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program, which gives security providers access to the company's frontier models, on-site engineers and threat research to protect operational technology (OT)…

Read the full itemCollapse

On October 8, Anthropic presented the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program, which gives security providers access to the company's frontier models, on-site engineers and threat research to protect operational technology (OT) behind power grids, water systems and transport networks, and government systems. The program starts with a small cohort of providers.

Eleven founding partners are on board: Accenture, Booz Allen, CrowdStrike, Deloitte, Dragos, Hitachi, Insane Cyber, Nozomi Networks, Palo Alto Networks, PwC and Rockwell Automation. Alongside it comes OSS Scanner, an opt-in, free service in which open-source projects that sign up receive periodic scans from Anthropic's most capable models. Each finding comes with a proof of concept, an explanation and, where available, a suggested fix. The reports are model-generated and sent without human review, so some may contain errors, for example in severity ratings; Anthropic expects a true-positive rate above 90 percent. Axios notes that it is unclear how partners will test and deploy fixes without disrupting utility operations, and that Anthropic has not said whether partners get free model access or who pays the compute costs. Project Glasswing was earlier in the week merged into an expanded Cyber Verification Program.

For the Nordics: none of the eleven founding partners is headquartered in the Nordic region, but Accenture, Deloitte and PwC operate there. The kind of operational technology the program targets is found at Nordic grid owners, waterworks and industrial groups, and Anthropic says more partners and sectors will follow. It is an open question whether players such as Svenska kraftnät, Statnett and the Swedish Civil Defence Authority (MCF) will be part of the program, and how it relates to the NIS2 incident-reporting requirements.

Source: Anthropic, Axios

Chalmers has built an AI scientist that sets hypotheses, runs experiments and learns from the results

Researchers at Chalmers University of Technology, the University of Gothenburg and the University of Cambridge have built an AI system that formulates biological questions, proposes experiments, evaluates the results and uses…

Read the full itemCollapse

Researchers at Chalmers University of Technology, the University of Gothenburg and the University of Cambridge have built an AI system that formulates biological questions, proposes experiments, evaluates the results and uses them in the next step, in a closed feedback loop. Chalmers published the news on September 30.

The system was tested in systems biology on yeast, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, and was given extensive information on the organism's genome, metabolism and earlier research as its knowledge base. It builds on large language models and automated reasoning, linked to a laboratory component in which lab results steer the next experiment. The work builds on Ross King's earlier robot scientists Adam and Eve, and the study, Agentic AI integrated with scientific knowledge: laboratory validation in systems biology, is published in the Journal of the Royal Society Interface. The researchers stress that the system is not meant to replace people: Ross King says humans still set research priorities, interpret the wider scientific significance and ensure ethical oversight. Chalmers does not report how many hypotheses were generated or confirmed.

For the Nordics: the work is funded by the Wallenberg program WASP, the UK's EPSRC, the Chalmers AI Research Centre (CHAIR) and Formas, and is an example of Swedish AI research moving from decision support toward systems that produce knowledge themselves. When a system proposes and runs experiments, the question for institutions such as Chalmers, the University of Gothenburg, KTH and Karolinska Institutet is how results are to be reviewed and credited, and how much lab work in Swedish biotech and drug research such a loop could eventually take over.

Source: Chalmers, Evertiq

OpenAI began rolling out Intelligent UI in ChatGPT on October 7, together with a new GPT-6 model. The feature lets ChatGPT present parts of its answers as interactive elements, such as tappable buttons, task-specific calculators and editable charts and graphs. Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users got access on October 7, and users of the free tier and the Go tier followed on October 8.

According to OpenAI's internal tests, as reported by TechStartups, GPT-6 Instant starts answering web-search questions 44 percent sooner on average than GPT-5.6 Instant. The measure is when the answer begins to appear, not when it is finished. GPT-6 Extra High begins responding at about the same speed as GPT-5.6 Medium. According to TechStartups, paid tiers get GPT-6 Sol and free tiers get GPT-6 Luna, and the change applies only to the chat experience, not to the models in Work and Codex. OpenAI acknowledges that Intelligent UI is still a work in progress, especially in how the system chooses layouts and interactive elements, and ChatGPT has more than 1.2 billion weekly users according to TechStartups. The speed figures come from OpenAI; independent measurements are missing.

For the Nordics: in the Enterprise version, workplace administrators control access according to TechStartups, so it is Nordic companies and public bodies that decide for themselves whether to switch the feature on. For organisations under GDPR, such as banks like SEB and DNB and the public sector, it is an open question whether interactive elements generated in the conversation affect what data leaves the organisation. OpenAI has not addressed it in the sources we reviewed, and we have not found any assessment from IMY or Datatilsynet.

DNB cuts about 400 FTEs in its technology division – and names AI as the driver itself

DNB announced on October 6 at 09:00 in a stock exchange release that staffing in the Technology & Services division will fall by about 400 full-time equivalents (FTEs).

Read the full itemCollapse

DNB announced on October 6 at 09:00 in a stock exchange release that staffing in the Technology & Services division will fall by about 400 full-time equivalents (FTEs). The bank itself states that agentic AI has been introduced in several parts of the business and that AI agents can deliver substantial efficiency gains, with customer data control, KYC work, and technology development and coding as examples. That is the bank's own wording, not an outside interpretation. Group CEO Kjerstin Braathen says AI is changing how the bank works and how it delivers services to its customers.

The figure is FTEs, not employees, and applies only to Technology & Services, not the whole bank. At the end of 2025 the group had 10,603 FTEs and 11,649 employees according to its annual report, so 400 FTEs is just under 4 percent of FTEs. The downsizing is to be completed in the fourth quarter of 2026, while the full cost effect will show in the accounts only from the second quarter of 2027; no cost figure has been published. DNB has not reported any quantified AI gain and has not broken down the FTEs by function, so how much of the reduction AI explains is unreported. AI is also one of several rationales; the bank also cites better customer experiences, greater efficiency and customers' future needs. Whether this will mean dismissals or voluntary solutions has not been determined by the bank; the process is to follow applicable rules and take place in close dialogue with employee representatives. Finansforbundet deputy leader Arne Fredrik Håstein criticises the pace in E24: he warns against making AI a law of nature that automatically means fewer employees, and says all options for staff must be explored before anyone is dismissed.

For the Nordics: the sequence is sharp. On October 2 the Norwegian government and the social partners signed an agreement on responsible AI in working life, and four days later DNB announced 400 FTEs would go, with AI as the stated driver. Whether the agreement is meant to cover a case like this is an open question, and events following one another in time are not the same as cause and effect. For Sweden, the DNB announcement becomes a reference point that SEB, Nordea and Finansförbundet can compare against. The EU AI Act regulates AI systems and their use, not individual staffing decisions.

Source: DNB, E24, Norwegian government

Anthropic releases Haiku 5.5 – a small model at a tenth of its predecessor's input price

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7. The model costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, against $1 and $5 for Haiku 4.5.

Read the full itemCollapse

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7. The model costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, against $1 and $5 for Haiku 4.5. According to Anthropic it is the company's cheapest, fastest and most capable small model, and it is available on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.

In Anthropic's own table, Haiku 5.5 scores 72.4 percent on OSWorld 2.1 (an offline subset), against 15.7 for Haiku 4.5 and 48.9 for OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna, and 45.9 percent on Humanity's Last Exam without tools. Sonnet 5.5 scores higher on both, at 83.9 and 56.9 percent. The company says Haiku 5.5 is on average about 75 percent cheaper to run than Haiku 4.5 (90 percent for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, 50 percent above that), and that the price of cache reads on Sonnet 5.5 has been halved from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens. All figures come from Anthropic and its customers, among them Asana, HubSpot and Box; independent measurements are still missing.

For the Nordics: a token price this low can make it economical to run small models at high volume, for example ticket triage, document reading and background steps in agents. That matters for Nordic software companies and IT consultancies building on Claude, such as Stockholm-based Lovable, and for banks and insurers such as SEB and DNB, where the volume of routine work is large. The price does not change data protection, however: IMY and Datatilsynet apply the same requirements to the handling of personal data regardless of model size.

Source: Anthropic

Finland orders Google to halt groundwork at two data centre sites

Finland's permitting and supervisory authority LVV on October 6 ordered Google's Finnish project company Tuike Finland to stop preparatory groundwork at the planned data centres in Muhos and Kajaani.

Read the full itemCollapse

Finland's permitting and supervisory authority LVV on October 6 ordered Google's Finnish project company Tuike Finland to stop preparatory groundwork at the planned data centres in Muhos and Kajaani. The halt must take effect immediately and no later than October 23, and last until the environmental impact assessments are complete. The company must respond in writing by October 14.

The halt covers tree felling, topsoil removal, excavation, blasting and crushing as well as site roads and storage areas, but not planning, measurements or soil surveys. The Finnish Association for Nature Conservation says more than 300 hectares of forest were cleared in Muhos, roughly 420 football pitches, before the impact assessment was complete. Google says it acted in good faith under the Forest Act, but has said it did not meet the high standards it sets itself and that it will comply. Otherwise the authority is considering enforcement, which can be backed by a conditional fine. The projects are part of a EUR 13 billion Finnish investment that Google calls its largest in Europe.

For the Nordics: the case is Finnish but the question is Nordic. A supervisory authority is stopping a hyperscale build during preparation, not once the building stands, and it is Google's communications manager for Finland and Norway, Sondre Ronander, who has answered for the company. Municipalities courting data centres, in Sweden for example Luleå and in Norway Skien, get a fresh example that clearing and groundwork can be halted before the environmental assessment is finished. It is a natural question for permitting authorities and municipalities in both countries: in what order do assessment and groundwork happen here?

OpenAI publishes 722 maths manuscripts – the results are not yet independently reviewed

In early October OpenAI posted 722 manuscripts on GitHub with solutions or progress on 372 major mathematical problems, produced by an as yet unreleased internal model.

Read the full itemCollapse

In early October OpenAI posted 722 manuscripts on GitHub with solutions or progress on 372 major mathematical problems, produced by an as yet unreleased internal model. According to OpenAI nearly every manuscript came from a single prompt to a single AI agent, and the average result required about three hours of ChatGPT Pro use.

OpenAI's claims include a solution to the four-dimensional Kakeya conjecture and progress toward the Riemann hypothesis. The advisory group of mathematicians AGMAI is linked to the release, but the results still have to be assessed by mathematicians, and scepticism persists after the earlier dispute over the Navier–Stokes claim. MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland says claims of single-prompt solutions should be treated as unverified until the model is released and the results can be replicated. OpenAI has disclosed the model's reasoning and estimates of compute, but withholds the prompts and the compute time per problem.

For the Nordics: the Abel Prize is awarded by the Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters in Oslo, and the mathematics departments at KTH, Chalmers, NTNU and the University of Oslo, among others, face the same question as colleagues abroad: how do you assess hundreds of machine-written proofs that nobody has had time to read? The Swedish Research Council and the Research Council of Norway may need clear guidelines for how AI-generated proofs are reviewed before they count as results. A first test will be which of the 722 manuscripts a Nordic research group can actually verify.

Source: Engadget, Scientific American
