What happens if it comes within a year or so and not decades?

I ask the question without knowing the answer. Neither does anyone else, and that is why it can be asked.

On November 10, 2026, AI Futures Project, the organization behind the AI 2027 scenario, published an update with the headline ”Q3.5 Timelines Update: Looks Like the Intelligence Explosion Will Happen in 2027 After All”. Daniel Kokotajlo, one of the scenario's originators and the most bullish of the authors, previously gave a 50 percent chance of Superhuman AI Researcher, SAR, before 2029. Now he writes, together with his co-authors: ”Now, all of us forecast that Superhuman AI Researcher (SAR) will likely arrive at some point in 2027 (assuming no substantial deliberate slowdown).” Same person, same document. By SAR they mean roughly the point at which a company would rather fire all its human researchers and rely entirely on automated AI researchers than keep the world's best human research team without AI.

But another number weighs more heavily. The authors write of the capability measurement that triggered the reassessment: ”even the most aggressive model-based distribution (Daniel's) assigned less than 4% to the pace of progress being sped up by at least 1.7x in November 2026”.

Under four percent. That is not a forecast. It is a forecaster who has put a number on his own miss, in his own source. If the one who calculated hardest gave under four percent to what happened — what else are we giving low numbers to right now?

Should one believe it? They barely do themselves. METR, an independent evaluation organization, states according to the same post that ”Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence we found most informative for these conclusions”. Kokotajlo himself: ”I still don't trust it completely, of course”.

On November 13 came Dwarkesh Patel's essay ”The avalanche that hit math is coming for AI development”, with the subtitle ”Why I now expect superintelligence by 2030”. In December 2025 the same Patel found it ”super implausible” that an automated researcher would crack the algorithm for AGI without having the basic learning ability that children have. Now he writes: ”If you asked me earlier this year how far away we are from superintelligence, I'd probably have said at least the better part of a decade.”

His median for when automated researchers are as good as the very best human AI researchers is 2028, provided the labs do not deliberately slow down. He gives a 25 percent chance that that point is not reached until after 2032. But it is the step after that which makes me stop. Automated AI research compresses, he writes, so much progress into a single year that shortly afterward we have AI at least as good as the best human experts in every field: ”so by 2030, but plausibly as early as next year”.

Plausibly. Not probably. But what if?

Take an accountant aged fifty-two. Who transitions her, and to what? Transition presupposes an occupation to go to, and that it is still standing when the training is finished. What do we say to her if we cannot point to it?

Take a child who starts school next fall. What should she learn? To read, to write, to do arithmetic. But then? School is built on our knowing roughly what world the pupils walk out into. What do we build school on when we do not know?

Take a trade union. It negotiates over how an incremental productivity gain is to be shared. What does it negotiate over when the gain is not incremental?

I am not asking in order to frighten. If automated research is real, the benefit is real too: diseases, materials, energy, the work no one should have to do. I want it. But the unpreparedness cuts both ways. A society that gets cures and abundance within four years, without having decided who gets what, is as unprepared as a society that gets mass unemployment. Are we ready? The question does not apply only to what can go wrong.

It can be tested. Sweden's AI strategy was presented in February 2026. The goal, according to the government's information page: ”Sverige ska vara bland de tio främsta nationerna inom artificiell intelligens (AI) i världen. Utgångsläget är starkt.” (”Sweden shall be among the top ten nations in artificial intelligence (AI) in the world. The starting position is strong.”) The strategy and its action plan have been read in their entirety — 24 and 11 pages — and searched for eleven terms, given here in the Swedish in which the search was made: superintelligens, artificiell generell intelligens, generell artificiell intelligens, AGI, intelligensexplosion, självförbättrande, självförbättring, rekursiv, automatiserad forskning, automatisering av forskning, förmågeexplosion (in English: superintelligence, artificial general intelligence, general artificial intelligence, AGI, intelligence explosion, self-improving, self-improvement, recursive, automated research, automation of research, capability explosion).

Zero hits.

The closest is a sentence on page 15 of the strategy: ”Oro över att AI kommer att användas på olämpliga sätt, att AI ska ha en negativ påverkan på arbete eller i framtiden leda till existentiella risker behöver tas på allvar.” (”Concern that AI will be used in inappropriate ways, that AI will have a negative impact on work or in the future lead to existential risks needs to be taken seriously.”) A concern to be taken seriously, then. Not a premise from which anything is planned. And in its consultation response to the Swedish AI Commission's Färdplan för Sverige (SOU 2025:12), LO noted on June 2, 2025 ”att AI-kommissionens uppdrag inte innefattade frågor kopplat till arbetsmarknaden eller skolväsendet” (”that the Swedish AI Commission's remit did not include questions related to the labour market or the school system”). The accountant and the child.

A curriculum takes years from consultation to classroom. A transition system takes a parliamentary term. A social safety net takes a majority. Count backward from 2027, from 2028, from 2030. The answer comes out the same. That is why the year does not matter, and that is why no one needs to believe in it. Neither do I.

I may be wrong, and that can be shown. The search was made in Sweden's AI strategy and its action plan, in their entirety, for the eleven terms, and returned zero hits. Show me the page where any of them stands as a premise for the planning — not as a concern, not as a list of technologies — and this essay falls. I would gladly see someone do it.
